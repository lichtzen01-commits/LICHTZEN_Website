FAQ

How do 254 nm UV-C and 222 nm Far-UVC differ?

254 nm UV-C is strongly absorbed by microbial DNA and disinfects powerfully in a short time, but it also affects people, so it is used only in unoccupied rooms. 222 nm Far-UVC is mostly absorbed in the skin's outer dead-cell layer, giving a skin TLV of 479 mJ/cm² (10 mJ/cm² for 254 nm), so it can be used continuously in occupied rooms.

Is it safe to run the Wonder-Ray® 254 nm disinfector while people are in the room?

No. The 254 nm disinfector must run only in unoccupied rooms. If the 360° motion sensor detects a person or animal, lamp power is cut immediately even during exposure, and the emergency stop button can halt it at any time.

How long does one disinfection cycle take?

The right exposure time is calculated by simulation for each room; 30 minutes or more is recommended. In in-house testing to BS 8628:2022, just 15 minutes gave a 6.0 log reduction for S. aureus and 5.0 log for E. coli.

Does it produce ozone during disinfection?

Wonder-Ray® is designed to block the specific wavelengths that can produce ozone. The ozone level measured in the KTL test (2024-12-10) was 0.007 ppm, about 12% of the 0.06 ppm indoor limit.

What certifications and tests does it have?

Wonder-Ray® holds KC conformity registration (R-R-LU0-WonderRay-W12), a KTR disinfection efficacy test (TBK-2024-007080), KTL airborne virus, airborne bacteria and ozone tests, utility patent 10-2771631 and design patent 30-1329825.

What is the warranty?

The unit has a 1-year free service warranty and the lamps a 5,000-hour warranty. 222 nm Far-UVC lamps can last up to about 8,000 hours depending on conditions.
